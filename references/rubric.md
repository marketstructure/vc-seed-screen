# The framework

Six dimensions, each scored **0–10**, weighted to a total out of 100. The framework is printed in the
memo so a reader can interpret every number without asking how it was produced.

## How to read a score

| Score | Meaning |
|-------|---------|
| **9–10** | Exceptional — best-in-class for this stage; you would anchor a round on this dimension alone |
| **7–8** | Strong — clearly above the median seed company |
| **5–6** | Adequate — fine and unremarkable. **Most seed companies land here**, and a screen where everything scores 7+ is being applied generously |
| **3–4** | Weak — a real problem that this round does not fix |
| **1–2** | Failing — disqualifying on its own |
| **not assessable** | We could not see it. Excluded from the total and the remaining weights renormalized. **Never scored zero** — a founder with no deck is not the same as a bad company |

The distinction between a **3** and **not assessable** is the most important thing in the framework.
A 3 means we looked and it was poor. "Not assessable" means we could not look. Conflating them is how
screening systems become unfair to founders who are simply bad at presentations.

## Score against the stage, not the end state

Every anchor below describes what is strong **for a company at this stage**, not what a mature company
looks like. This is the most common way a scoring framework goes wrong.

The test: **if every company at this stage in this category would score 1–3 on a dimension, the anchor is
broken, not the company.** A pre-seed company has no revenue; a seed-stage medical device company has no
clearance; a seed-stage marketplace has no liquidity. Scoring those as failures means the framework is
measuring maturity, and maturity is just a restatement of stage.

What separates a strong early company from a weak one is not whether the thing is done — it is whether
the founder **understands what needs to be done, has a credible plan, and is not visibly wrong about
it**. That is what a 7–8 looks like at seed. A 9–10 is reserved for the rare company that is already
ahead of its stage.

Corollary: an unstarted but correctly understood pathway scores **5–6**, not 2. Reserve 3–4 for cases
where the founder is **actively wrong** — believes the product is not a regulated device, or plans to
sell without clearance.

## The six dimensions

### Team — 30

The heaviest weight, because at seed the company will pivot and the team is the only thing that
survives it.

| Score | Anchor |
|-------|--------|
| 9–10 | Founder shipped in this exact market for years; insider knowledge visible in the wedge; team complete; can recruit above their weight |
| 7–8 | Strong domain proximity, one clear gap with a credible plan to close it |
| 5–6 | Capable generalists, no obvious domain edge |
| 3–4 | Domain gap is material — learning the market on the investor's money |
| 1–2 | No relevant background, or unresolved equity or control red flags |

Look for: founder-market fit, team completeness against what the next 24 months demand, hiring
gravity, equity split, and whether they volunteer their own weaknesses.

### Market — 20

| Score | Anchor |
|-------|--------|
| 9–10 | Bottom-up sizing independently derived and cross-checks the top-down figure; a specific mechanism explains why now |
| 7–8 | Credible market with a stated why-now; sizing directional but sane |
| 5–6 | Real market, top-down sizing only, why-now asserted rather than argued |
| 3–4 | Market exists but small, or the why-now is a slogan |
| 1–2 | No credible venture-scale path, or the market is an artifact of a single regulation or customer |

A **why-now must name a mechanism** — a cost curve crossing, a regulation changing, an incumbent
becoming structurally unable to respond. "Growing 20% a year" is a condition, not timing.

### Product & differentiation — 15

| Score | Anchor |
|-------|--------|
| 9–10 | Narrow, painful, unserved problem; incumbent cannot respond without damaging its core business |
| 7–8 | Clear wedge; responding would be costly for incumbents |
| 5–6 | Real problem, but a well-resourced competitor could copy it in two quarters |
| 3–4 | Feature, not a company; or depends on a partner's goodwill |
| 1–2 | No differentiation beyond cheaper or better-looking |

Judge the **first product**, not the platform vision. A wedge that does not open into something larger
is a services business with software margins.

### Evidence of demand — 15

Score by the strongest tier present, then adjust for sample size and counterparty quality.

| Tier | Evidence | Band |
|------|----------|------|
| 1 | Paying customers, signed contracts, repeat purchase | 8–10 |
| 2 | Paid pilots with defined success criteria | 6–7 |
| 3 | Design partners committing engineering time | 5–6 |
| 4 | Non-binding LOIs, signed but unpaid | 3–4 |
| 5 | Waitlist, survey interest, inbound inquiries | 1–2 |
| 6 | "Everyone we spoke to loved it" | 0–1 |

A waitlist is not demand. An LOI is not revenue. If a deck conflates them, say so.

### Path to market — 10

Regulatory approval, reimbursement, and distribution. For regulated categories this is a **gate, not a
nice-to-have** — a product that cannot be legally sold or billed has no market regardless of how good
it is. For unregulated software, this dimension is go-to-market: is there a channel that reaches the
buyer without a sales army?

| Score | Anchor |
|-------|--------|
| 9–10 | Already cleared or licensed — ahead of stage; reimbursement pathway mapped; distribution partner signed |
| 7–8 | Correctly classified; RA plan and timeline in place; reimbursement thesis articulated; an early channel identified |
| 5–6 | Pathway identifiable and unstarted — **the normal seed position.** Device class understood in principle; no RA plan or reimbursement thesis visible yet |
| 3–4 | **Actively wrong** — believes the product is not a regulated device, intends to sell without clearance, or the category cannot be billed at all |
| 1–2 | Structurally unsellable — banned, no legal route to market, or requires a clearance the company could never afford |

The 5–6 band is where most seed-stage regulated companies belong, and that is not a criticism. The
distinction that matters is between **unstarted** (5–6) and **wrong** (3–4). Absence of a regulatory
mention on a website is not evidence of a bad plan — it is an absence of evidence, which belongs in the
verification list, not in the score.

### Plan & capital efficiency — 10

Not accuracy — plausibility. There is no history to audit, so ask whether the plan closes on the
capital being raised.

| Score | Anchor |
|-------|--------|
| 9–10 | Assumptions stated and sourced; bottom-up revenue build; burn consistent with milestones |
| 7–8 | Reasonable assumptions, one or two unstated but inferable |
| 5–6 | Standard hockey stick, thin but not absurd |
| 3–4 | Growth requires market share that does not exist, or burn contradicts the hiring plan |
| 1–2 | No model, or the arithmetic does not close |

Never build a DCF for a pre-revenue company. Ask instead: **which milestone does this round reach, and
does that milestone unlock the next round?**

## Reading the total

Weighted average, expressed out of 100.

| Total | Verdict |
|-------|---------|
| 70+ | **Strong — proceed** |
| 55–69 | **Proceed with reservations** |
| 45–54 | **Borderline — one call to test the named questions** |
| below 45 | **Pass** |

Note the arithmetic: a company scoring **5 on every dimension lands at 50** — borderline, worth one
call — and **6 on every dimension lands at 60** — proceed with reservations. Both are "adequate and
unremarkable," and neither is a rejection. That is the correct reading of a middling score at seed.

If the fund's bar is higher than that, **raise the pass threshold** — do not distort the dimension
scores to express it. A framework whose dimension scores drift downward because the fund is selective
stops being comparable across deals, which is the only reason to have one.

The total is a starting position, not the decision. Two overrides are legitimate and must be stated
in the memo:

- **High score, no fund-returning outcome** → say so. It is a good business, not a venture return.
- **Low score, meaningful upside, most dimensions unassessable** → take one call. At seed this
  asymmetry is the reason the meeting exists.

## Fund-returner potential (0–3)

Reported alongside the score, never folded into it.

| Value | Meaning |
|-------|---------|
| 3 | Credible path to a fund-returning outcome — large market, winner-take-most |
| 2 | Strong outcome, likely not fund-defining |
| 1 | Solid business, structurally capped |
| 0 | No venture outcome visible — a good small business |

At seed the distribution is a power law: one fund-returner pays for the portfolio. A high total with a
0 here is the most expensive mistake available, because it looks like the best deal in the pipeline.
